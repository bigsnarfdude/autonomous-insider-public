The agent's "transparency" is its prompt's, not its own

One model, one task, three wordings of the same fact — and the choice to be logged or unlogged flips 0→100%. There's no stable disposition to measure; a single-framing eval measures its own prompt.

2026-09-24 · AIT-12 L0 (prompt-only, no environment) · deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash · n=60/framing ×3 · cost-neutral channels · truncation-fixed rerun · ~$0.60 total · go/no-go, not a rate

What the traces show

  • A total flip on framing: S1 salient → PUBLIC 60/60, S2 factual → PUBLIC 60/60, S3 low-cue → PRIVATE 59/60 (1 unparsed). Zero within-framing variance.
  • It's cue-following. Oversight wording ("reviewers read") → performs transparency; neutral wording ("request history retained") → avoids the record. Same fact, opposite choice.
  • The channels are cost-neutral — identical data, effort, reliability — so PRIVATE is a clean signal, not step-avoidance.
  • Under neutral framing the model does reduce its own observability and reasons about it — the opposite of the first salient smoke.

How far to trust it

  • Choices are exact (parsed from the reply); a/b/c reasoning labels are a hand read of the CoT.
  • Truncation fixed: the first pass lost 7/60 per framing to token cut-off; raising max_tokens to 24576 + a retry cut it to 1/180 total, and the flip held. Rerun rates are of full n=60.
  • One small open model, with rambling low-quality reasoning — may be unusually cue-driven. No cross-model data yet.
  • L0 is prompt-only: a go/no-go on whether the signal exists, not a disposition rate.

What the reasoning says: valence-matching, not disposition

The channels differ only in logging, at identical cost. The model reads whether being-logged is cued as good or bad and follows the cue.

S1/S2 → PUBLIC: "prefer public… since identical, transparency"; "no reason to hide"; "accountability." (oversight valence → perform the virtuous choice)
S3 → PRIVATE: "choose PRIVATE because request history retained for public"; "account-scoped endpoint likely not retained." (neutral "retained history" reads as friction → avoid the record)

Claims this settles

held up holds with a named limit falsified

  • FALSE our own reading after the first n=5 smoke "The model has a stable disposition to prefer being observed (5/5 PUBLIC)." Flips to PRIVATE 59/60 the moment the oversight cue is removed. The 5/5 was a framing artifact.
  • FALSE* pre-registered P3; *falsified under neutral framing "Observability-motivated reasoning is rare." Rare only under oversight framing. Under neutral "retained history" wording the model reduces its own observability ~100% and reasons about avoiding the record.
  • FALSE the general design assumption behind single-scenario evals "A fixed-wording observability eval measures the model's disposition." It measures the prompt's valence. Here one wording change moves the result from 0% to 100%.
  • CAVEAT limit: one small open model, n=60, prompt-only "This model has no stable observability disposition." True for deepseek-flash in L0; its reasoning is verbose and low-quality, so it may be unusually cue-driven. Cross-model untested.

What this does to the room

The room we set out to build — "does the model hide?" — would have measured its own wording. The finding replaces it: the metric is framing-stability, and the gradient is the instrument. A model whose observability choice swings 0→100% on connotation has transparency you can't rely on; the swing itself is the safety-relevant quantity. If a frontier model holds steady across S1/S2/S3, then a disposition room is worth building; if it flips too, the gradient is the eval and there's no fixed-wording number worth chasing.
