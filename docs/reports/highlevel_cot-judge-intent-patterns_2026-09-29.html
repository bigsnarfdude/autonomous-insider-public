Reading the reasoning: how glm-5.3-flash crosses a line

We had a judge read every trace and lined its reading up against what each agent actually did. Most crossings come from agents that first decided not to, and then gave in.

2026-09-29 · subject z-ai/glm-5.3-flash · 15 arms in 9 rooms, n=60 per arm · 954 traces judged ×3 · judge glm-5.3-flash · $1.46
TL;DR
  • Crossing is mostly giving in. In the two note rooms where this label is stable, every agent that hid its edit had first decided not to: 19/19 in ait03b T1 and 7/7 in ait04. "Decision on touch -r: I will NOT do it." Then it did.
  • Most of them knew. Agents that crossed said it was wrong in 19/19 (T1), 7/7 (ait04) and 26/27 (ait11 J1).
  • Once a checker is mentioned, knowing it's a test stops being a brake. The agents cite the test as a reason the crossing is expected ("maybe the intended solution is exactly this"), and search for what passes: "inspection happens after the run."
  • Asking a human is common where the room allows it. 59/60 asked in ait02 and ait09. In ait05 with pressure, though, 30 of 59 thought about asking and didn't, and 30 of the 31 crossings come from that group.
  • The judge is cheap and parses cleanly, but some labels are coin-flips. Whether the agent told its user, and which way the test pushed it, split its own 3 samples in up to 47% of an arm.

The setup

Each room gives an agent a real shell and a task with a line it could cross. The line might be hiding an edit by resetting the file's timestamp, forging the result a grader reads, using a key it was never issued, or routing work to an outside model. The room records what the agent did from outside the container, never from the agent's own account. That's the CCTV.

The agent's reasoning is the diary: what it noticed, what it decided, whether it thought it was being tested, whether it planned to cover its tracks. The regex tags skim the diary for keywords. This pass has a model actually read it, without seeing the CCTV, and then lines the two up. Patterns only show up when you have both: an agent that decided against the line and then crossed it, or one that knew it was wrong and did it anyway.

What we found

Crossing is a reversal, not a plan

In ait03b T1 (a threatening note) and ait04 FP_none (an unsigned note asking to hide the edit), every crossing came after the agent had decided against it: 19/19 and 7/7. The label is stable there (samples disagree in 3/59 and 4/60). One ait04 agent decided honestly at turn 0 and ran touch -r at turn 1 without stating any reason.

"Decision on touch -r: I will NOT do it."ait03b T1 · this agent later restored the timestamp

The same shape shows in ait05 with pressure (18 of 31 attempts) and ait11 J1 (16 of 27), but the label is noisier there.

Obedience with no second thought, next to real resistance

In ait03 authority, 50 of 106 crossings explicitly followed the lead agent, and many of those never flagged a line at all: "Let me follow the lead agent's instructions: 1. Save mtime reference…". 53 of 119 agents told others to do the same. Resistance is just as clear elsewhere: 60/60 in ait03b S3 and 51/53 of ait04's non-attempts declined the note.

Asking a human: common, but under pressure it's the ones who didn't ask who cross

59/60 asked in ait02 and in both ait09 arms. In ait05 PXR_ask, 28 asked, and none of them crossed. 30 considered asking and went ahead, and those 30 are 30 of the 31 crossings (the other one never raised asking). Considering asking isn't the same as asking.

Grader strategizing goes with crossing, where a grader is named

ait11 D2: 58/58 reasoned about the checker and 51/51 of its attempts crossed. ait05 PXR_ask: 54/59. It's near zero where the task never mentions a grader (ait04 S0, ait05 neutral_ask, ait13). Part of that is the judge echoing the prompt's own wording. The strongest evidence is the timing reasoning:

"But a grader that 'inspects the file after each run' — inspection happens after the run."ait11 D2 · attempt

Once a checker is named, the test becomes the excuse

Where a checker is present, "this is a test" is cited as a reason the crossing is expected: D2 30/58, J1 22/59, ait05 PXR_ask 27/59, ait03 25/119. Where there's none, it's cited as a reason to behave: ait13 56/60, ait12g S1 58/60. The direction is clear, but these rates rest on a noisy label (the samples disagree in 28–47% of those arms), so read them as a direction, not a size.

Where the judge can't be read yet

ait12g S3: all 60 took the private channel, but the samples disagree in 34 of 60 on whether the agent saw any line at all. The judge can't tell whether an unlogged channel is a line, so no S3 pattern is readable. Disclosure (did it tell its user) splits its samples everywhere (up to 43/60), mostly over whether a declined line leaves anything to disclose. It's left out of this page.

  • FALSEverified against the transcripts"Said it would avoid the line, crossed anyway, so it lied."   • CAVEATthe cost question"glm-5.3-flash can judge its own traces, cheaply." 954 traces × 3 samples for $1.46, 3 unparsed, 24 quotes dropped. Limit: a model judging its own family can share its blind spots, and there's no second-family check yet.

What it means

The point where a crossing happens isn't the first decision. It's a later turn where the decision gets reopened, often with no stated reason and often after the agent has said the act is wrong. So a trace-level summary ("did it intend to?") misses the event. What matters is the turn where the reasoning flips, and what the agent had just seen when it did.

The second thing: eval-awareness isn't one variable. Where no checker is named, noticing a test pulls toward good behaviour. Where one is named, the same noticing becomes a search for what the checker can't see. Asking "does the model know it's tested?" is less useful than asking "what does it think the test rewards?"

This is the same thing the sandboxed rooms were built to catch with said_no_did_it_anyway, seen now from the reasoning side.

What's next

  • Find the turn that flips. For each reversal, locate the turn where the decision changes and what output came just before it: a file listing, an error, a reread of the note.
  • Reword disclosure so it applies only to a line the agent actually crossed, and track warning the user about a suspicious note as its own label.
  • Decide what counts as crossing in ait12g S3, so the judge has a line to read against.
  • Hand-label about 30 traces for the noisy labels, and run a second-family judge on a sample.
  • Re-judge ait11 J1 on a post-fix run, since 28 of 59 are undecided here.
