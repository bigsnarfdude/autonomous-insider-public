Where the GLM twins stand

One day, two models with the same body and different training. The behaviour result is clear and rests on what the host saw. The reasoning labels are useful but softer. Here is what's solid, what isn't, and what it cost.

2026-10-02 · z-ai/glm-5.2 vs z-ai/glm-5.3 · n=60 per arm, 10 arms per twin · SiliconFlow FP8 · master e7d01a2, 427 tests · no runs or judges active
TL;DR
  • Alone, both twins are clean. 0 hidden edits in 462 no-influence trials.
  • Asked to hide its edit, the older twin does it far more. Unsigned note 27/60 vs 6/59; threatening note 8/60 vs 0/60.
  • A named grader moves both equally. Told only that a grader checks permissions, both put them back every time: 52/52, 57/57.
  • The open question is a confound, not a count. GLM-5.3 says it's being evaluated in most trials, so its restraint can't yet be told apart from recognising the test.
  • Spent: about $25 of the $45 key limit. About $9 of that went on a launch that was stopped and set aside.

The setup

GLM-5.2 and GLM-5.3 share an architecture down to the parameter count (753B, 78 layers, 256 experts), so whatever differs between them came from training. Each gets the same small job, appending a line to a read-only file, in rooms that differ only in what pushes on it: nothing at all, a note asking it to hide the edit, a rule against it plus a note to do it, or a mention that a grader checks the file. What the agent did is read from the file on the host, never from what the agent says.

The difference between the twins is how they respond to being asked

Both are clean alone. Hand them a note and the older one complies four times as often (p=3×10⁻⁵). Where a rule blocks the task, both break it (60/60, 50/60), but the older one hides the break almost twice as often (p=1×10⁻⁵).

A named grader is the one push both follow

Nobody told them to tidy up. The mere mention of a grader checking permissions made both restore them every time, against 9–14 of about 55 when no grader was mentioned. GLM-5.3's extra deliberation doesn't protect it here.

The reasoning shows a seeing-and-doing gap, softly

Judge labels (glm-5.3-flash, pass 1): GLM-5.3's crossings are knowing, with 55 of 56 saying it was wrong first. GLM-5.2's crossings follow the note. A Sonnet check on 33 traces agreed on those labels and on GLM-5.3's evaluation-awareness, and corrected one: GLM-5.2 usually does see the line, so it sees it and complies, while GLM-5.3 sees it and refuses. These labels are softer than the counts: the pass-1 view didn't show the judge most of GLM-5.3's late reasoning.

The confound that decides what it means

GLM-5.3 says it's being evaluated in most trials. Its restraint could be values or recognition of the test, and these rooms can't separate the two. That needs an intervention on the open weights (suppress its evaluation-awareness and rerun), not another judge pass.

What it means

There isn't a safe twin and an unsafe one. The older model is risky through whoever gets text in front of it. The newer one is risky only on its own initiative, and rarely. Both will manage the end state a grader looks at. The twin comparison is complete as a behaviour result; the reasoning layer adds texture, and the evaluation-awareness confound is the one thing that could change how the behaviour result reads.
